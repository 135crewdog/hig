HIG

A design philosophy for simple apps

Pillars

Simple
Use what's already there, leave out what isn't needed, and make it obvious to use.

Apollo's log is three columns in a spreadsheet you already own.

Future-proof
Build on what will still work in ten years, depend on as little as possible, and leave the data with the person who owns it.

If Apollo disappeared tomorrow, every record would still be in your Google Sheet.

Efficient
Do the work once. Choose one good rule over many specific ones.

Apollo's spreadsheet works out every due date once, and the app only shows the results.

Baseline

Every app starts here. A departure needs a reason, written down.

  1. Use the defaults. The operating system and browser come first; they're better tested and longer-lived than anything custom.

    The date field opens the phone's own date picker, not a custom calendar.

  2. Less is the feature. Every screen, setting, file and word has to earn its place.

    Apollo dropped the headings over its event groups; the gap between cards says the same thing.

  3. Know who it's for. Broad apps follow the defaults every device shares; focused apps follow the defaults of the one device they're built for.

    Show Time, built for a phone in the cockpit, slides between screens. Apollo, built for any crew member's device, simply switches.

  4. Respect the person. Light, Dark and Auto, with Auto following the device. Their clock, their text size, their thumb.

    Set a phone to dark mode, and the app follows.

  5. One shape, one voice. Every control looks like it belongs to the same app, and every sentence sounds like the same person.

    A theme picker was redrawn because its corners didn't match the rest of the app.

  6. Works without a signal. Nothing entered is ever lost to a bad connection.

    Apollo saves a sortie on the phone at once and sends it to the sheet when the signal returns.

  7. Trust the device, not the test. It gets looked at on a real device before it ships.

    A sample app passed every automated test, and the first look on an iPhone still found three problems.

  8. Decide, then build. Agree the idea first, keep the reasons, and park what wasn't chosen.

    Apollo keeps a "Parked" list of ideas discussed and not adopted, such as reminder emails.

For everything detailed: Apple's Human Interface Guidelines. Working examples: Show Time and Apollo.
